With `defaultBody` seeing args, the guard exit just does `brk 0 [.reg acc]`. This is exactly MLIR `scf.while`'s before-region / Cranelift's block-param shape, and the zero-trip case falls out for free (`args = inits` on the first check). **Main suggestions/criticism:**

1. **Don't fork the compiler — lower SSA → Prog.** This is the headline. With Prog's unbounded registers, out-of-SSA is nearly syntactic: each `cont` edge is a parallel copy into the args registers, and the classic lost-copy/swap hazard dissolves by staging through fresh temps (registers are infinite). Register pressure stays in Prog's future allocator where it belongs. LowIRSSA's right home is the first rung of the higher-IR ladder (Ext. 5), and the entire ProgSim campaign gets reused unchanged.
2. **What SSA genuinely buys for proofs:** loop invariants become statements about the args tuple read off the `cont` sites, and outer-name immutability becomes a once-proved SSA frame theorem instead of per-proof "all other registers unchanged" bookkeeping.
3. **Severable pieces worth taking even without SSA:** valued `ret` (kills the rets-register convention — but touches the live ProgSim ExecFacts/StmtSim files, so post-campaign), `.const` operands, and `.never` typing as an Ext. 8 `wf` extension.
4. **Honest costs:** textual def-once forbids sibling `ife` arms reusing a name; `mayBrk` over-approximation admits some dead code; the checker has more moving parts than Prog's `wf`, which a soundness proof pays for once.
